# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary — Hospital & diagnostic-lab procurement.** Purchasing officers, laboratory
managers, and administrators at Philippine hospitals and diagnostic labs who are
sizing up whether LabSolution is a supplier worth engaging. They arrive cautious and
risk-averse, mid due-diligence, often comparing distributors before a formal inquiry
or tender. They are scanning for proof: which manufacturer brands LabSolution carries,
how deep and legitimate the catalog is (regulatory standing, real spec sheets), and
whether the company has a real footprint. Job to be done: decide, quickly and with
confidence, that LabSolution is a serious, dependable partner — then reach out or
remember them at decision time.

**Secondary — Resellers & sub-distributors.** Downstream dealers who might carry
LabSolution's lines into smaller markets. They're evaluating the same thing from the
other side: is this a credible principal whose portfolio is worth representing?

This is a B2B trust audience, not a consumer-conversion one. They skew toward older,
busy professionals who value track record over novelty and read for substance.

## Product Purpose

The marketing site for **LabSolution Technologies, Inc.** — a Philippines-based
healthcare/diagnostics distributor. Its job is to **establish credibility**: present
the partner manufacturer brands and the Documented Catalog of instruments, analyzers,
and tests, show the company's locations and history, and convert that earned trust into
qualified sales inquiries. Conversion is the contact form, but credibility is the real
product — success is a procurement officer or reseller leaving convinced LabSolution is
legitimate and dependable, and acting on that when a purchasing decision comes due.

## Brand Personality

**Established · reliable · institutional — kept human.** The site should carry the
stability and authority of a long-standing diagnostics distributor: the safe,
dependable choice with a real track record. But "institutional" must never tip into
cold, faceless corporate (an explicit anti-reference). The existing "helping you help
people" thread is the humanizing counterweight — a quiet mission-led warmth that keeps
authority from reading as sterile.

Voice: assured, plain-spoken, evidence-led. Confident without bragging; no hype, no
growth-hacky urgency, no jargon padding. Quiet confidence, because to this audience
restraint reads as expertise.

## Anti-references

- **Templated WordPress / WooCommerce.** The generic plugin-built look of the old
  `labsolution.ph` site — stock theme, widget clutter, e-commerce chrome. (Note: a
  stale auto-scraped `labsolution-design` skill still describes that old site —
  Poppins/Lato, 5px grid, WooCommerce tokens. It does **not** reflect this codebase
  and should not be treated as the design source of truth.)
- **Cluttered medical-supply catalog.** Dense spreadsheet-style product grids, tiny
  text, everything competing for attention at once. Curate, never dump.
- **Cold, sterile, generic corporate.** A faceless stock-photo template with no point
  of view — forgettable and impersonal.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust through substance, not flash.** Credibility comes from real partner
   brands, catalog depth, regulatory legitimacy, and physical footprint. Surface those
   proofs; don't decorate around them.
2. **Institutional, but not faceless.** Project the stability of an established
   distributor while keeping the human, mission-led warmth. Stability + humanity —
   never cold corporate.
3. **Calm hierarchy over density.** Procurement readers scan for confidence signals;
   lead them through one clear idea at a time. Curate the catalog, don't spreadsheet it.
4. **Quiet confidence.** Restraint reads as expertise to this audience. Let whitespace,
   typography, and one disciplined accent carry authority — avoid hype gradients,
   animated metrics, and other consumer-tech tells.
5. **Every claim should be checkable.** Names, locations, certifications, partner
   logos — concrete proof beats adjectives for a risk-averse B2B buyer.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal conformance target (best-effort). Preserve and build on the foundation already
in the code: the global `prefers-reduced-motion` override, the skip-to-content link,
visible focus rings, and semantic landmarks. Aim for AA-ish contrast and full keyboard
support as a matter of craft without committing to a formal WCAG level. Because the
procurement/reseller audience skews older, calm motion, legible type, and generous tap
targets are good instincts here regardless of the absent formal bar.
